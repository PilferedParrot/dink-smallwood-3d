# Dink's interface in first person — 2026-10-07

Direction by GPT-6.1 Sol (high effort), accepted by Dink lead Claude Opus 5.5 on 2026-10-07. GPT-6 Astra reviewed planning and the integrated presentation; a separate blind Astra session reviewed the export.

Chris's complaint about 0.3.0 is the brief: the interface looks generic, is hard to read intuitively, and does not look like Dink. Restore the original game's visual vocabulary while making every action readable to a first-time player.

**Reference and materials.** Use the original imported `Startme` title/logo, `inter/status/stat-03.png` stone status bar, `inter/Menu/menu-01.png` equipment chest, item icons, and `inter/Text-box` marble and vine artwork. Keep their original pixels and proportions; do not invent fantasy ornaments. These existing assets are distributable under the original data pack's zlib-style license, preserved in `licenses/FREEDINK-DATA-COPYRIGHT.txt`; they are not CC0. Any additional artwork will be ours or CC0. Original Dink used platform-rendered type rather than an included font: retain readable engine type for instructions, without introducing a new font asset. Never claim a substitute is the original font.

**Title.** Restore the illustrated original title instead of the village-preview/green-panel combination. Use a clear vertical list: Continue when available, Begin adventure, Settings & controls, Credits & support, Quit. Put a brief first-person control primer beside the list. Preserve full keyboard, pointer, and controller focus.

**Adventure.** Anchor the recognizable stone status bar at the bottom, with weapon and spell pictures, the original stat colors, life and magic meters, coins, level and experience. Add readable labels and numbers, keep original icon art at its aspect ratio, and expose equipment/pause controls. Leave the center view clear; show talk/confirm prompts where the player needs them. Scale within 1280×800 and widescreen without truncating hints.

**Menus and dialogue.** Carry the marble, stone, brown recesses and vine borders into pause/settings/journal/credits and dialogue. Replace poetic page titles with direct labels. Dialogue keeps the speaker and scene visible, with a conspicuous Continue action and keyboard/controller hint. Equipment uses named, illustrated choices; empty spell slots say how to obtain magic. Focus must be indicated by shape/border as well as color; text must have solid, contrasting backing. Settings retain text size and reduced camera movement.

**Boundaries.** This unit owns `game/scripts/game_ui.gd`, new UI-only resources, this document, UI capture tooling and focused UI checks. It leaves `fp_world.gd`, scenery/prop/fence/building code and campaign scripts to the lead. Existing action signals, input mappings, saves and gameplay rules remain the integration contract.

**Evidence and acceptance.** Capture title, adventure HUD, dialogue, equipment, pause and settings from Linux exports before and after. Label any fixtures separately from normal input progression. Inspect actual frames, including text at the supported size extremes. Use isolated saves/settings/logs and software rendering when GPUs are busy. Run one blind top-model player round on the exported build, using Pinelle, Wong & Stach's CHI 2008 heuristics (read from the local research PDF; it is not shipped). Fix serious interface findings and record deferred gameplay findings with reasons. Commit a verified local unit only; publishing needs Chris's authorization.


## Implementation and review decisions — 2026-10-07

The original title was composed on black with the wooden logo (original `Story/START.c`, sequence 196), so the adaptation restores that logo on a dark backdrop with named actions. Marble text panels and original vine sides unify menus and dialogue. The chest is functional: filled original slots are focusable, equipped slots have an E badge, a full-size description names the selected item, and additional magic slots use a second page. The bottom dock keeps the status artwork's 8:1 ratio and leaves the lower side views open. Location remains visible above the scene.

The existing Godot fallback font remains the dynamic-text face; it is **not** represented as Dink's original typeface. [FreeDink's font research](https://sources.debian.org/src/freedink/109.6-1/doc/fonts.txt/) documents the original's Arial-based game text and FreeDink's Liberation substitute. The imported pack contains raster labels/digits but no arbitrary-text font. No new font or external art was added. The original art and license notice remain intact.

Two Astra review passes found a wrong starter-bow icon and page-entry scrolling that hid headings/Return. Fallback icons were audited against original campaign `add_item`/`add_magic` registrations, replacing guessed mappings; the starter bow uses `item-w08`, pig feed `item-w02`. The blind player additionally found nested Esc Back unexpectedly resumed play and notifications lost contrast against a tan wall. Parent-menu navigation now routes through the existing action signals, and notifications have a solid dark backing. Keyboard/mouse cues are the initial default; actual controller input switches them. These choices address Pinelle et al.'s predictable actions, readable status, discoverable controls and interpretable representations.

The blind player found E aimed at Mother initially selected the fireplace; moving closer recovered Mother's conversation. The interaction target/range cue is queued to the Dink lead because its target-selection behavior belongs to the gameplay/world integration. Outdoor progression, real controller hardware, audio and a complete campaign are outside this bounded interface review; no certification of those follows from the menu checks.

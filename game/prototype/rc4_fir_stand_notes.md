# RC4 fir stand, final isolated attempt — 2026-10-10

**Verdict: negative on the measured boundary-spacing requirement.** The lead's final rule permits standing trees only where source forest art and hardness coincide, or where an original hard sprite box supports a trim part. The fitted whole fir's crown is 94 source pixels wide, but 12,200 of 38,401 full-resolution forest-hard-edge pixels across 38 screens remain farther than half that width from any admissible tree root. On a 4 px sampling grid the same result is 3,091 of 9,264 points. Screen 144 has 420 of 1,545 uncovered full pixels (maximum 89.308 px to a root); screen 375 has 221 of 590 (maximum 148.224 px). A full-pixel refinement covers every pixel that *any* admissible root could cover: the exact lower bound is also 12,200, with zero avoidable gaps after fitting. The source custom hard masks extend into neighboring grass and at times have no forest-art-and-hard pixel close enough to plant a tree. The JSON records a SHA256 of each boundary pixel mask, its largest uncovered component boxes, unowned hardness components and spacing residual. No new collision or standing tree on walkable forest was added to erase this mismatch. This is the second and last layout attempt; the root owns the visual gate and the decision to stop.

## Reproduction and ownership

`tools/rc4_fir_stand_fit.py` regenerates `game/prototype/rc4_fir_stand.json` for **all 76 outdoor screens** with ts01 forest cells and **all 69 outdoor screens** with static tree-09/10 placements (81 screens in the union). `game/prototype/rc4_fir_stand.gd` provides `load_fit`, `fixture`, `suppresses_sprite`, `replacement_ground`, and `build`. The compiler input has source hashes for `world.json`, `sequences.json`, and `ts01.png`.

The atlas at x200–399,y200–399 shows four foreground fir crowns in sixteen 50×50 cells, one transferable density of 0.25 crowns/cell. The original complete fir is seq32/frame1. Its drawn crown occupies x34–127, **94 px wide**, measured above its root with alpha>127 and non-shadow RGB sum>40; its root is at (83.5,176) in the sprite. Every generated tree uses that original frame at 0.025 m/source pixel, `fp_world.clean_texture`, `sprite_foot`, and `shadow_twin`. Global 100×100 world cells and a fixed SHA256 seed choose interior positions; only source-hard forest-art pixels may hold roots. Across the world, this yields 9 interior density trees plus 297 firs along forest-connected tile-hard edges. Where the hard boundary runs through grass masks beyond a crown's reach of source-hard forest art, the fit records uncovered edge points.

The old tree-09/10 art is two stacked parts. All 123 static placements were inventoried with exact source screen/list index/editor index, frame, type, hard flag, vision, parts, and seam/coincident ownership. All old placements are listed for precise suppression, including seven type-0 background sprites; those must be filtered **before ground painting**. Of shown source parts, 39 have source forest tile hardness or an original hard sprite box at their drawn root pixel and become complete firs there. Another 89 are suppressed as walkable clearing with explicit source member, part, foot, tile-hard distance, sprite hardbox distance and original-art cost. Parts already hidden by seam or coincident ownership get no extra tree. Screen 375 editor index 1's lower part becomes a whole fir at local foot (392.5,143). Source vision/type matching is required by `suppresses_sprite(fit,screen,raw_editor_e,source_list_index)`; an index match alone cannot suppress another story layer or sprite type.

The source tile hardness and entity hardboxes are **unchanged**; cutouts add no collider. A connected-grass pixel mask clears only owned ts01 forest art to source grass donors. Art with no source forest hardness becomes walkable ground with no standing trees, as the lead directed. This clears 156,101 forest-art pixels on screen 144, 26,903 on screen 375, and 7,359,310 across the fixture set. That sacrifices part of the original-camera canopy and is an explicit cost of aligning visual solids with the authored mask. Screen 439 has no fixture and is an unchanged half-tree control. The runtime does not paint or suppress anything on a screen outside the JSON.

## Both causes of failure

The first fir attempt's technical defect was a row of apparent openings along hard forest edges: area density alone does not cover a boundary. We made it by applying a density heuristic to an edge task; random macrocell roots projected crowns outside the screen or behind the collision edge. The final attempt instead uses the fir's own 94-pixel crown width and source tile-hard contour.

The remaining defect is in the source relationship between the artwork and its custom hard masks: hard forest-connected components can extend into grass cells farther than a 94-pixel crown can cover from a tree rooted on both forest art and hardness. Screen 375's source-hard art occupies y129–147, while its hard edge runs x350–375 from y1–123. We did not infer missing tree footprints from those grass-hard pixels because the lead's final rule forbids trees on forest-looking walkable art or arbitrary nearby hardness. This is a source-data limit exposed by the rule, not a height or density value that can be tuned per screen. The JSON itemizes 412,059 hard pixels near forest that cannot be attributed to the forest-connected source component across 56 screens; some may belong to other structures and must remain unclaimed.

Prior art checked: the project already has `collision_map.tile_mask`, `facade_fit` member tracking, `fp_world` seam, ground and sprite/shadow mechanisms, and `seam_objects.py`'s independent account of stacked parts. Those are the mechanisms used or ported. [PyDink](https://github.com/wijnen/pydink) documents original Dink tile/sprite layers but supplies no depth reconstruction. [Tian et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05772) use multiple photographs for forest reconstruction and report canopy/ground uncertainty; their method supplies no depth for this single atlas. No external asset was downloaded.
